Builder's log · 2003 — 2026

Twenty-three years of shipping, with the receipts.

For most of my career I was a design-led PM: I could see the product clearly, could tell you when a few pixels were off, but I needed a team to write the code and build it.

Since December 2025 I consider myself, first and foremost, a builder — designing, coding and PMing at once, shipping daily and sometimes hourly. Below is every chapter, what I claimed, and what the numbers actually said.

2003 — 2010 · London

Don't Stay In

Co-founder

Co-founded a social network from a flat in London, built the viral loop, grew it to a top global site, sold it.

2MMonthly uniques
12MPhotos
20MForum posts
15Team hired
6Years to exit

The loop was the product: photographers shot the club night, the pictures went up by morning, and by lunchtime everyone was back tagging themselves and arguing in the forums. Go out, get photographed, come back to look. It is still the cleanest viral mechanic I have worked on.

Under it we built the business half — a back end where promoters ran events, engaged their communities, and bought banner ads and email campaigns. Six years in: two million people a month, an office, fifteen staff, and an exit.

Spotify

Product Manager, spotify.com · Premium Manager, Subscriptions

Employee ~200. Rebuilt sign-up, premium and download through hypergrowth, and kept the team shippable while the company grew 20×.

I owned spotify.com — sign-up, premium subscription, download. The flows everybody touches and nobody thinks about. We rebuilt them on real user research, then again for mobile, then again for a company that no longer looked like the one that hired me. Millions of new registrations came out of that work.

The harder problem was organisational. As the stakeholder list grew, I introduced a thoughtful execution model so prioritisation stayed honest and decisions did not turn into committees.

Two jobs at once. One: lead the website team, supervise a new CMS, and rebuild product pages and funnels alongside growth marketing. Two: establish a new product development team aimed at professional certification exams — a line that did not exist, for people retraining mid-career.

The thing I would put first is not on the org chart. I mentored a direct report from associate PM to senior, and grew two senior designers alongside them.

Note

Products get sunset. People keep going. This is the chapter that convinced me the management half of the job was worth being good at.

2020 — 2022 · New York

Senior Product Manager

6Disciplines in the room

Success in this job is defined by absence: traffic does not dip, SEO does not wobble, no editor's day gets worse. There is no launch moment to celebrate.

So we forecast it honestly and sequenced it with design, data, tech, SEO, accessibility and content in the room from the start. It landed, and nobody wrote about it — which was the point.

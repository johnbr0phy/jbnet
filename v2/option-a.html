John Brophy · Builder & Product Leader

Twenty-three years of building things on the internet.

From a social network run out of a London flat to an AI landing-page platform in New Jersey. Six chapters, the work, and the numbers.

Scroll ↓

For most of my career I was a design-led PM. I could see the product clearly, could tell you when a few pixels were off, but I needed a team to write the code and build it.

Since December 2025 that has stopped being true. I now consider myself, first and foremost, a builder — designing, coding and PMing at once, shipping daily and sometimes hourly. Have an idea in the morning, get it live by lunch.

Don't Stay In

Co-founder

Higgsfield 02 · 3:2Queue outside a UK club in the rain, 2003, disposable-camera look, wet pavement reflections, hoods up, one photographer's flash

2004. The whole product was one loop: go out, get photographed, come back to look.

I co-founded a social network from a flat in London, before anyone was calling them that.

We were obsessed with club nights, so we built dontstayin.com for them. Photographers shot the night; the pictures went up by morning; by lunchtime everyone was tagging themselves and arguing about it in the forums. That loop was the entire engine — and it is still the cleanest viral loop I have ever worked on.

Behind it we built the boring half that made it a business: a back end where promoters ran their events, talked to their communities, and bought banner ads and email campaigns. Six years in we were a top global social network, we had an office, and we had grown the team to fifteen. Then we sold it.

Spotify

Product Manager, spotify.com · Premium Manager, Subscriptions

Higgsfield 03 · 3:2Golden-hour office window reflection, 2012, MacBook with soft green glow, shallow depth of field, city beyond the glass, warm and quiet

Seven years. At that growth rate, that is four different companies.

I joined at around employee 200 and stayed seven years, while the company grew twentyfold around us.

I owned spotify.com — sign-up, premium subscription, download. The flows everybody touches and nobody thinks about. We rebuilt them on real user research, then rebuilt them again for mobile, then again for a company that no longer looked like the one that hired me. Millions of new registrations came through that work, and the account-management section got its first properly mobile life.

The harder problem was human. As the stakeholder list grew, everything threatened to become a committee, so I introduced a thoughtful execution model: clear communication, honest prioritisation, and a team that stayed shippable through hypergrowth.

Stensul

Product Director

Higgsfield 07 · 3:2Overhead desk at midnight: two monitors, terminal open, coffee, warm lamp, dark room — the nights-and-weekends builder

Now. Somewhere in this chapter the job changed for me.

Zero to one — and then I learned to build it myself.

I took a beta landing-page builder and turned it into an enterprise platform: brand books, custom modules, user-level guardrails, all of it grounded in more than fifty customer discovery calls. End-to-end creation time fell to about an hour, customers doubled the number of pages they were making, and it added real ARR.

We also led the build of a Model Context Protocol server, putting Stensul in the middle of how marketers will reach their tools through AI as MCP becomes table stakes. The other half of the job has been teaching the organisation to build with AI — prompt architecture, agentic workflows, a discovery process rebuilt around them.

And somewhere in there the job changed for me. I stopped being the person who describes the product and became the person who builds it. Everything on the Current Projects page came out of that shift.
